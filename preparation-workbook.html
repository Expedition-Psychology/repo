Preparation · Controllables and uncontrollables

The Preparation
Workbook

Work through what an event will ask of you, one demand at a time. For each one there is a single question, and the answer decides what you do next.

Before you start

One question, asked over and over

Worry is the mind scanning ahead for problems. That is useful, but only if each problem it finds gets an answer. Left alone it loops, turning the same difficulty over without reaching a conclusion.

This workbook gives each difficulty somewhere to go. You list what the event demands, and then ask one question of every item on the list: is there something I can do about it?

Step one

What is the event?

A date in the diary tells you when you need to be ready. It doesn't tell you what being ready involves. Start by naming the thing you are preparing for.

Step two

What are the demands?

Everything you think will be hard about it. Work outwards: you, then the event itself, then the surroundings. Stick to what is likely and important — you don't need every imaginable disaster.

Why this step

This is where you put the worrying part of your mind to work. Instead of letting it circle vague "what ifs", ask it to write the list. Once a difficulty is on paper it stops being a dread and becomes something with a next step.

Step three

Controllable, or not?

Take each demand in turn and ask the one question. Don't agonise over it — if you can picture an action that would genuinely change the thing, it's controllable. If the action would only change how you meet it, it isn't.

A demand of the event
Is there something I can do about it?
Yes · Controllable

Find a solution, then plan it in

Something you can organise, practise, learn, pack, book or ask for.

For example: not knowing the people you'll be relying on → get in touch, meet them beforehand, agree what you need from each other.
No · Uncontrollable

Practise in it

The weather, the schedule, other people, the dark, the cold, how your body reacts.

For example: having to perform cold and short of sleep → train in exactly those conditions, until you know what they do to you and what helps.
Why the second branch works

"Uncontrollable" sounds like nothing can be done, and that is usually where people stop. But there is a difference between changing a situation and being ready for it.

You can't make the night shorter, the water warmer or the room less full. What you can change is how much of it is still unknown when you get there. Riding through the night a few times beforehand doesn't make the night any easier. It tells you exactly what happens to you at three in the morning, and what to do about it.

Every repetition answers a question you would otherwise be carrying to the start. How bad does this actually get? What helps? Can I keep going anyway? Once those have answers, you are no longer hoping you will cope. You know that you can, and you know how.

That is what it buys you on the day. Attention spent wondering what is coming and whether you will handle it is attention not spent on performing. Practise the conditions beforehand and that part is already settled, so the day itself has room for the thing you actually came to do.

Step four

A plan for each one

Every demand gets a plan — the branch it took decides what kind. "I'll get better at it" is an intention. A plan has a when, a where and a how.

Uncontrollable — practise in it instead

For each of these, two things: how you will get yourself into those conditions beforehand, and the routine you will use once you are in them. Practise in the real thing wherever you safely can — it is the closest match to the day, and it answers the most questions. Where that isn't safe or possible yet, build up to it rather than skipping it.

Step six

Reflections

This part is for later. Once you have actually done one of these, come back and write down how it went. Leave the rest blank until their turn comes.

Why this step

Practice only tells you something if you stop to notice what it told you. A couple of lines after each attempt is what turns "I did it twice" into "I know what works, and I know how much more I need."
