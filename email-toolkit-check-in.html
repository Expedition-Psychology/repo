Some people are most of the way through by now. Others have done two chapters and not opened it since. Both are completely normal, and neither tells you much about how it will turn out.

Consistency matters more than intensity

The best predictor of whether this works isn't how hard you push in any single session. It's how often you come back.

Fear doesn't unlearn itself in one heroic afternoon of falling practice. It updates slowly, through repeated experience that the thing you braced for didn't happen. That needs repetition, spread out. A few minutes of deliberate practice most sessions will do far more for you than an occasional hour that leaves you rattled.

If you've been climbing but skipping the exercises, pick one and attach it to something you already do — the first route of every session, or the last ten minutes before you leave. Deciding in advance where it goes is most of the battle.

A bad session isn't a reset

Progress here isn't a line going up. You'll have sessions where it feels like you've gone backwards, and they often arrive just after a run of good ones, or when you're tired, or at an unfamiliar wall with people you don't know.

That's the ordinary shape of this work, not evidence that it isn't working. Log it, note what was different about that day, and carry on. The log is there precisely so that one rough session doesn't get to rewrite the story of the last month.

If you're stuck, say so

If an exercise feels like too big a step, if something isn't making sense, or if you've stopped and can't quite work out why — just reply to this email and tell me. It comes straight to me and I read all of them.

Needing the steps broken down smaller is not a sign that this isn't for you. It's the most common adjustment there is, and it's usually a five-minute conversation.
