Setting Goals

A practical, evidence-based workbook for turning meaningful goals into a plan you can actually live.

About this workbook

From what matters to what you'll do this week

Big goals rarely stall because we don't care enough. They stall because they stay vague — a distant wish with no obvious first move. This workbook closes that gap.

You'll choose a few goals that genuinely matter to you, picture what success looks like six months on, then break each one into small weekly actions and a concrete plan. 

What you'll leave with

A plan.

Clear goals, and specific actions scheduled into your week.

How it works

Small actions, real momentum

Several options for each week means progress stays possible even when circumstances change.

How to use this workbook

1

Work through the four steps in order. Each one builds on the last, moving from what matters to a plan you can start on this week.

Direction

Identify Meaningful Goals

Choose three to five goals that genuinely matter to you — not the ones you feel you should pursue. Goals rooted in your own values are the ones you'll keep moving towards when motivation dips.

Need a starting point?

Here are some goals other people have worked on. 

List the goals that matter to you. For each, add a line on why it matters — the value or hope underneath it. Star the two or three you'd most like to work on first.
You don't need five. Two or three goals you truly care about will carry you further than a long list you feel lukewarm about. The starred goals are the ones you'll carry through the rest of this workbook.
02
Vision

Define Progress

Picture yourself six months from now, having made real progress. A vivid, specific picture of progres is one of the strongest sources of motivation there is — so let yourself imagine it in detail.

As you imagine

Take one focus goal at a time. Close your eyes for a moment and step into that future version of your day. Answer from inside the picture — present tense, as if it's already here.

03
Momentum

Build Weekly Actions

For each goal, list several small, realistic things you could do this week. Having options — not one fixed task — means you can still make progress when life gets in the way, and it keeps things varied and interesting.

Worked example — Become more confident

—  Attend a social event

—  Start a conversation with someone new

—  Practise positive self-talk each morning

—  Record one small achievement each evening

—  Watch or read something about building confidence

Completing any one of these is real progress. You're not aiming to do them all — you're keeping momentum in the right direction.

04
Your plan

Create Your Action Plan

Now turn your chosen activities into a plan clear enough to actually happen. The more specific you are about when and where — and how you'll handle what gets in the way — the more likely you are to follow through.

Plan one activity to start

Choose one activity to commit to this week, and set out exactly when and where you'll do it. That single, specific action is enough to build momentum — you can add more activities below whenever you're ready.

Put your plan in your calendar

Download an .ics file of every activity you've given a day and time, then open it to add them straight to your phone or computer.

Come back whenever your week changes. Adjust an activity, add a new one, and download a fresh calendar file — a plan is meant to flex around your life, not the other way around.

You don't have to feel ready to begin. Take the first small action, and let the motivation follow.

Return to this workbook whenever you want to re-plan, add a goal, or refresh your week. Your direction is always here to come back to.
