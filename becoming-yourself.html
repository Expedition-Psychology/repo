About this workbook

Choosing your direction

This workbook helps you look forward, and describe the person you want to keep becoming.

There is an important idea underneath everything here: a value is a direction, not a destination. Kindness, courage, curiosity — you never “arrive” at them. You move towards them, choice by choice. Goals can be reached and ticked off; values are the compass heading you keep travelling in.

So this is not a blueprint you have to achieve, and it is not about designing one perfect future and then measuring yourself against it. The picture you build here is a heading, not a finish line. If you set off in a direction that genuinely reflects your values, then whatever the outcome, you’ll know your choices were your own — made for you, not for other people’s expectations or the pull of habit.

That is the real aim: not to arrive at an exact life, but to spend more of your days walking towards the person you actually want to be.

Grounding

Looking Back to Look Forward

Before you look ahead, get clear on what already matters to you. Your values, strengths, and the moments you feel most yourself become the foundation for where you head next.

Keep these answers in mind as you go. Everything that follows is about heading further in the direction these already point.
02
Direction

Picturing the Person You're Becoming

Imagine yourself further down the road, living in a way that feels true to you — not a perfect life, just a truer one.

As you imagine

Don't worry about being realistic, impressive, or precise. You're not committing to anything or predicting the future. You're simply pointing at a direction — noticing what “living in line with my values” would actually look and feel like.

Remember — this is a heading on a compass, not a place you have to reach. You can start moving towards it tomorrow, in the smallest of ways.
03
Life domains

The Areas of Your Life

A whole life has many sides to it. For each area that matters to you, picture what you'd like it to look like — and the value that picture expresses.

How to fill this in

Not every area will feel equally important, and that's fine — some may be blank. For each one, picture what you'd like that part of your life to look and feel like, and note which of your values it connects to. You're describing a picture, not setting a fixed target.

For each area that matters to you, picture what you'd like it to look like — and the value underneath it.
Relationships & connection the kind of partner, friend, family member you want to be
Work & contribution the way you want to show up in what you do, and what you want it to stand for
Hobbies, interests & play what you want to make room for, purely because it’s alive and yours
Health & self-care how you want to look after your body and mind, out of care rather than punishment
Learning & growth what you want to keep stretching towards or becoming better at
Community & the wider world the contribution you want to make beyond yourself
04
Obstacles

What Pulls You Away

Notice — gently, and without blame — the habits and pressures that quietly steer you away from the person you want to be. Awareness is what loosens their grip.

A kind reminder

The point of this section is not to list your faults. These patterns are things that happen to all of us — often they're old ways of coping that once protected us. You're not looking for reasons to feel bad; you're building awareness, so that next time you can notice the pull and choose differently. Read every answer here with the same kindness you'd offer a good friend.

Tick any of these common “hooks” — the patterns that tend to pull you away from yourself. There's no shame in any of them; almost everyone will recognise several.
Add any of your own that aren't listed here.
05
The path

Choosing Your Direction

Bring it together into a clear picture of the person you want to keep becoming.

The idea

This isn't about a perfect plan or a fixed destination. It's about getting clear on what you'd like your life to look like — so that when choices come up, you can tell which way is genuinely yours.

If you take a wrong turn, you haven't failed — you've just noticed you're off course, which is the first move back onto it. The direction is always there to return to.
Bringing it together

Your Direction, In Your Own Words

Using everything you've reflected on, write a short, honest statement of the direction you're choosing — the person you want to keep becoming, and the values you want to travel by.

Consider including

—  The values you most want to guide your choices from here

—  The directions you want to move in, across the areas of life that matter to you

—  The kind of person you want to be for the people around you

—  The hooks and pressures you want to notice and gently step around

—  A reminder to yourself that this is a picture to keep returning to, not a finish line

Aim to answer one question: Who am I choosing to become — and what direction will I keep moving in, regardless of exactly where I arrive?

You don't have to arrive to be on your way. Every choice in a valued direction is already the journey.

Return to this workbook whenever you drift, or whenever the road ahead feels unclear. Your direction is always there to come back to.
