Would you tell me how it went?

Two months in. If the Toolkit changed something about your climbing, hearing that from you is worth more to the next climber than anything I can say about it.

It's been a couple of months since you started the Fear of Falling Toolkit, and I'd like to ask you a favour.

Climbers deciding whether to do this course don't really want to hear from me. I'm the one selling it. What they want to know is whether somebody who was where they are — talking themselves out of routes, dreading the clip, backing off things they could physically climb — actually came out the other side.

That's the one thing I can't tell them. Only you can.

What I'd ask you

There are eight questions: where you started and what the fear was costing you, whether there was a moment something shifted, what you can do now that you couldn't before, and what you'd say to someone thinking about the course.

It takes about ten minutes, and you can skip any question you'd rather not answer. A few honest sentences are worth far more than a complete set of polite ones.

Your words stay yours

Nothing you write gets used anywhere without your permission, and you choose how you're named — full name, first name only, or completely anonymous. If I'd like to quote you, I'll send you the quote first so you can see it in context.

And if you change your mind later, it comes down. No explanation needed.

If it hasn't worked, I'd rather know

If the honest answer is that the course didn't do what you hoped, please don't quietly delete this. Reply and tell me what happened instead. That feedback is more useful to me than another good review, and it's how the course gets better for the next person.
