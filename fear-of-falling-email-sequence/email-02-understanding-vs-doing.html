Knowing it isn’t the same as doing it

You watched the talk, so the four strategies make sense on paper. What happens on the wall is a different question — and that gap is not a knowledge problem.

Thanks for watching the talk. I hope the four strategies landed.

Here is the thing I see most often, and it has nothing to do with whether you understood them.

You are two clips up. The next move is a long reach to a hold you cannot quite see, and the bolt is level with your hips. You know the fall is clean. You could probably explain, right now, exactly why your body is reacting the way it is.

And you still shuffle your feet, chalk up twice, grip harder than the hold needs, and come down.

Why understanding does not transfer on its own

Fear does not update because you have been told something is safe. It updates through repeated experience that the thing you braced for did not happen — at a level you can actually tolerate, often enough for the learning to stick.

That is slow, specific work, and it is where most people stall. Not on the theory, but on the practicalities: which exercise, at what level, how often, and how you would know you were ready to step up.
