Trauma symptom check (PCL‑5)

Twenty questions about how a very stressful experience has been affecting you over the past month. It takes about five minutes. You will get a total score, a breakdown across the four symptom clusters, and a plain-English note on what the numbers usually mean.

Looking after yourself

Answering questions about a traumatic event can stir things up. If you feel shaken, put the screen down, get your feet on the floor, slow your breathing, and contact someone you trust. If you are struggling badly or having thoughts of harming yourself, talk to someone now: call the Samaritans free on 116 123 (24 hours), contact your GP, or dial 999 in an emergency.

This is a screening questionnaire, not a diagnosis. Only a qualified practitioner can assess post-traumatic stress disorder, and a score cannot do it for them. If your score is at or above the cut-point, or if you are worried whatever the number says, speak to your GP or a trauma-informed therapist.

PCL‑5 developed by Weathers, F. W., Litz, B. T., Keane, T. M., Palmieri, P. A., Marx, B. P., & Schnurr, P. P. (2013). The PTSD Checklist for DSM‑5 (PCL‑5). Available from the US National Center for PTSD at ptsd.va.gov.
